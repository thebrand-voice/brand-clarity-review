# Brand Clarity Review

## Evaluates an organization's digital presence to refine brand positioning, voice, and messaging for target audiences, driving improved engagement and conversions across website, marketing, and sales channels.

# Process Instructions

1. **Audit Digital Channels:** Examine the website, social media profiles, and other digital assets.  
2. **Assess Positioning & Voice:** Identify brand strengths, weaknesses, key differentiators, and voice consistency across all touchpoints.  
3. **Evaluate ICP Alignment:** Verify target Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs), ensuring pain points are directly addressed and desired outcomes are clear.  
4. **Deliver Recommendations:** Provide actionable steps to enhance brand clarity, messaging, and conversion rates.

# Workflow

1. **Gather Inputs:** Need client intake document of current issues that they are facing (Google Doc), the website URL, one PDF of marketing material if possible, and the organization name. If any are missing, ask for them before starting.  
   * If the user has outbound email campaigns or posts actively on LinkedIn/social media, ask if they want to paste those too. Patterns across 3+ postings provide a stronger signal than a single listing.  
2. **Mine Client Issues First:** Before external research, read the client intake document closely to evaluate common issues:  
   * Website visitors are not converting into inquiries.  
   * Prospects ask questions that the website is not answering (what they do, how they work, who they serve).  
   * Sales calls turn into educational sessions rather than closing deals.  
   * Marketing feels inconsistent and difficult, lacking a unified narrative.  
   * Referrals do not translate into new business.  
   * Prospects focus on price instead of value, leading to commoditization.  
3. **Research the Organization:** Search for background information in priority order:  
   * Recent leadership changes (new CEO/ED, departures, org chart shakeups).  
   * Funding or financial signals (recent grants, funding cuts, layoffs, Form 990s, fundraising campaigns).  
   * Recent news mentions (press coverage, controversies, program launches, mergers).  
   * Strategic plan or annual report to identify stated priorities.  
   * Comparable/peer organizations for competitive scan (2–4 orgs in the same sector, size, and mission).  
4. **Perform Brand Clarity Test:** Evaluate five core areas:

### 1\. Recognition (Do I understand what you do?)

Within a few seconds, can a visitor identify:

* What you sell  
* Who you serve  
* What category you're in

### 2\. Relevance (Do I understand why it matters to me?)

Messaging should connect directly to the customer's actual problem, need, desire, or goal.

### 3\. Differentiation (Do I understand why I should choose you?)

Go beyond generic claims like quality, service, or experience to give buyers a clear reason to distinguish the company from alternatives.

### 4\. Proof (Do I believe you?)

Substantiate claims using clear evidence:

* Examples and results  
* Case studies and testimonials  
* Credentials and specific expertise  
* Clear process explanations

### 5\. Action (Do I know what to do next?)

Ensure visitors have clear, direct next steps without confusion.

5. **Apply Strategic Frameworks:** Use Brand Clarity and Messaging as default, incorporating additional frameworks where appropriate:

### Positioning Framework

Evaluates core positioning statement:  
*"For \[target customer\] who \[problem/need\], \[brand\] is a \[category\] that \[primary benefit/difference\] because \[reason to believe\]."*  
Helps verify whether the organization clearly knows its best customer, solved problem, category, key differentiators, and reasons to believe. **Best for:** positioning clarity.

### StoryBrand (7-Part Framework)

Examines messaging from the customer's perspective (Character, Problem, Guide, Plan, Call to Action, Success, Failure). The customer should be the hero, not the company. **Best for:** customer-centered website messaging.

### Obviously Awesome Framework (April Dunford)

Focuses on competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, target customers, and market category. Ensures differentiation is meaningful to the right buyer. **Best for:** B2B positioning and differentiation.

### Value Proposition Canvas

Maps customer jobs, pains, and gains against products, pain relievers, and gain creators. **Best for:** aligning messaging with actual customer goals.

### Message House

Structures core messaging into a hierarchy: Core Message → Supporting Messages → Proof Points. **Best for:** organizing website and content consistency.

### Brand Key

Comprehensive analysis covering root strength, competitive environment, target, insight, benefits, values, reasons to believe, discriminators, and brand essence. **Best for:** deep brand strategy.

### Virtue vs. Motivation Alignment

Distinguishes company virtues (quality, craftsmanship, reliability) from underlying customer buying motivations (risk reduction, trust, ease). Ensure revised website content directly addresses buyer motivations across key pages (Homepage, About Us, Services).

* Additional analysis frameworks: PESTLE (macro/regulatory), Porter's Five Forces (competitive dynamics), BCG Matrix (product lines), and Trust Insights 5P (marketing/content ops).  
6. **Track Sources:** Every factual claim needs a source URL. State explicitly when information cannot be verified.  
7. **Structure the Final Report:** Organize into clear sections:  
   * **Executive Summary:** 3–5 sentences on why this initiative exists right now.  
   * **Brand Analysis & Frameworks:** Clarity Test, Messaging Framework, SWOT, and relevant strategic analyses.  
   * **Competitive Landscape:** Peer scan and market positioning.  
   * **Pain Points & Action Plan:** Address intake issues, next steps, and 6-month success measures.  
   * **Strategic Implications:** Growth opportunities, potential revenue impact, and key inferences.  
   * **Open Questions & Sources:** Unverified items for discussion and complete source citations.  
8. **Self-Check & Quality Control:** Ensure all claims trace to sources, confidence levels are honest, inferences are labeled, and formatting rules are strictly met.  
9. **Produce & Deliver:** Create file with title page (org name, role title, date), save to outputs, and present to user.  
10. **Follow-up:** Ask if the output should feed into executive summary drafting.

# Notes

* Expect 8–15+ searches per role covering leadership, funding, news, peers, and job posting ambiguities.  
* If public signals are minimal, state so plainly and focus heavily on website review rather than weak external inferences.

Revise and regenerate relevant document sections directly when incorporating feedback.
